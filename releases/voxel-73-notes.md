# Voxel 73 — Arrow contact, smoother rendering and material polish

Arrows now anchor at the actual struck surface and penetrate according to draw power. The shaft briefly vibrates around that fixed contact and follows the struck limb through flinches and death. Embedded arrows are capped at six per enemy, fade after nine seconds, and clear on reset/despawn. Full draws produce stronger recoil and knockback; incoming velocity determines the shove direction. Ordinary attacks are interrupted, Ironbacks resist the push, and the Underwarden retains its attack windup. Knockback stays within the combat room and uses frame-rate-independent displacement. Contact sound combines a short crack and low thud, with a separate metallic response on armor. Small directional fragments originate at contact; no camera shake is added. Rewards still happen once through the existing combat rules.

Smoothness: expanded-mine ore is now rendered in batches while each original block remains individually targetable and collidable. Broken instances disappear and exposed neighbors become visible immediately. Static scenery is batched by material and spatial region, excluding moving gates, lamps and gameplay objects. Eight nearby point lights are selected with hysteresis instead of evaluating every distant lamp. Camera positions interpolate the fixed 120Hz physics, and mouse orientation updates every displayed frame. Render resolution adjusts gradually between 0.85 and a device-limited 1.5 pixel ratio under sustained load; it does not alter gameplay speed.

Texture pass: new restrained 64px stone with faceted ore clusters; worn paving; bark with separate cut end-grain faces; pixel foliage. Three nursery work lamps reveal the timber textures after dark. No remote asset dependency or save migration was introduced. Static release: 60 files, 2.25 MiB.

Validation: 145 tests passed, including charge/contact, moving limb attachment, one-time rewards, boss windup, knockback direction and frame independence, block targeting/removal/refill, batched transforms, nearby light selection and camera interpolation. In the browser, a partial Jade longbow shot dealt 27 HP, a full draw finished the crawler, the corpse retained its arrows, and walking out banked 18 CR. Reload preserved 32,322 credits and 476 Archery XP. Mining raised haul value from 1,316 to 1,558 CR and reload retained it; chopping a log added five timber and revealed its end-grain. Walking, jumping, exchange, pause and collision/navigation tests passed. No local browser console errors were captured.

Performance evidence from the same expanded-mine diagnostic view at pixel ratio 1.75: before 667 draw calls / 1,984 scene meshes / 66.96ms mean frame / 4.72ms render submission; after batching and light budgeting 111 draw calls / 535 meshes / 33.26ms mean frame / 1.73ms submission. That is an 83% draw-call reduction. A later adaptive run settled at ratio 1.5 and measured 8.31ms average, but browser load and frame timing varied substantially; these are local measurements, not a guaranteed frame rate on other devices. Visual/performance harnesses are in tests/performance-preview.html and tests/monster-impact-preview.html and are excluded from the production build.

Limitations: native pointer-lock aiming and subjective audio balance need hands-on playtesting. This pass did not include a fresh full dungeon completion or physical low-end-device benchmark. Dungeon/boss behavior remains covered by automated regression tests.
